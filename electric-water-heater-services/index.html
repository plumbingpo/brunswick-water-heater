Electric water heaters fail in a few very specific ways, and the symptom usually tells you where to look. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A tank that delivers a few minutes of hot water and then runs lukewarm suggests the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. Those are the calls we answer most in Brunswick, GA, and nearly all of them are fixable.

Electric water heaters are easy to test when you know what to measure, and that is exactly how Brunswick Water Heater starts. At each Brunswick home, our technicians measure element resistance, check for elements shorted to the tank, test thermostat operation, confirm the breaker and supply voltage, and inspect every connection from the junction box to the terminals. We also look at sediment. A lower element buried in mineral buildup runs hot, fails early, and can make the tank noisy, so whenever we pull an element, we check how much sediment is sitting on the bottom and flush it out if needed.

Once we know what failed, the fix is usually quick. Elements, thermostats, and high-limit switches are standard parts, and our trucks carry the common sizes. Replacing an element means shutting off power, draining the tank, installing the new element, and refilling completely before power is restored, which protects the new element from a dry fire. Damaged wiring, loose terminals, and sediment on the tank bottom are handled in the same visit. If replacement makes more sense, we install new electric heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, confirm the breaker and wire gauge are correct, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code requires. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A red reset button that keeps tripping
  • Crackling noises as the heater runs, often from sediment
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a hot smell at the wiring
  • Rusty water, leaks around an element, or water at the base
  • Hot water that runs out after just a few minutes
  • A tank that has stopped heating completely
  • A breaker that trips when the heater calls for heat

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A typical electric water heater sits on a dedicated 240-volt, 30-amp circuit, which is reason enough not to learn on it. The thermostats sit right next to live terminals behind the access panel, and a meter probe that slips can cause a dangerous arc. Replacing an element also means draining the tank, and forgetting to refill it completely means the new element burns out the moment power returns. Symptoms also overlap. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
  • Drain a few gallons from the bottom of the tank periodically to control sediment
  • After any draining, refill the tank fully and run a hot faucet before restoring power
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • Treat a tripped high-limit as a warning sign, not a nuisance
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Set both thermostats near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
