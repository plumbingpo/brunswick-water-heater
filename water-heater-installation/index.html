Not every water heater job is a straight replacement. Plenty of Brunswick, GA projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Others are moving a unit out of a cramped closet and into the garage, upgrading to a larger tank for a growing household, or converting from one fuel to another. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Before any pipe is cut, we match the heater to your household. For tanks, first-hour rating counts more than gallons; for tankless, it is gallons per minute at your winter temperature rise. Then we pick the location with you. A gas model has to vent outdoors and draw enough combustion air. A heat pump model has to have breathing room, temperatures above roughly 40 degrees, and a condensate drain. Electric tankless models often require a dedicated high-amperage circuit. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water

Why Professional Water Heater Installation Matters

Start with the gas line. An undersized line starves a new heater whenever the furnace or range runs, and a whole-house tankless unit usually needs more gas than the existing line carries. The vent has to be the right material, size, and pitch, with proper clearances, so exhaust leaves the home instead of drifting back into it. Electric models bring their own math, since breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity all have to match. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. Our technicians plan the gas line, vent, and circuit before the first cut, take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one, and install the relief valve discharge and code items correctly. Every joint is tested, every gas install is checked for draft and carbon monoxide, and the temperature is set to protect against scalding. Rushed installs tend to leave undersized gas lines, bad vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks behind. Ours are done to code and to last. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
