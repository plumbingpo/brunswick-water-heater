Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

No homeowner likes a surprise on the invoice. So Brunswick Water Heater prices every job before the work begins, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

The trouble with water heater failures is that most of them start inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals build a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod wears down a little at a time, and when it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Brunswick Homes

A maintenance visit from Brunswick Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and sets a safe water temperature. Minor faults get caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your water heater keeps running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust heats water with less effort, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Compared with the cost of an early replacement, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. We don't simply copy the size of the old heater. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. We'll compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a well-sized tank can be the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

No hot water, a burner that refuses to light, a tank full of sediment, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Brunswick Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a thermocouple that no longer holds the pilot, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where pressure is climbing, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
