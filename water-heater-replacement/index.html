How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and plenty of households in Brunswick, GA tend to learn that the hard way, with a wet floor.

For tanks, that means looking at the first-hour rating, the amount of hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour, rather than tank gallons alone. With tankless, it is flow rate in gallons per minute at the temperature rise your water needs in winter. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. Our technicians match the new heater to your hot water demand, check that the gas supply, venting, and panel can handle it, and take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one. Relief valve piping is done right, applicable code items go in, leaks and draft are tested, and the old tank leaves with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
