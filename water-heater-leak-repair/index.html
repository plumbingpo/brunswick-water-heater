Where is the water coming from? That question decides whether a water heater leak costs a little or a lot. Relief valves weep when they wear out or when household pressure runs high. Drain valves, especially the plastic ones found on many factory tanks, start dripping as they age. Fittings on top of the tank corrode where copper meets steel, and flexible connectors fail at the gasket. Condensation can mimic a leak for weeks on a gas unit or in a humid space. And sometimes the tank itself has rusted through. In Brunswick, GA, we sort out which one you have before recommending a thing.

Finding the source is a process, not a glance. We clear and dry the area, then watch where new water appears. First we check the relief valve and the pipe it drains through, followed by the drain valve and the inlet and outlet fittings, where dielectric unions and flex connectors tend to corrode. If the heater is gas-fired, we look inside the burner compartment, since water there can be condensation or the first sign of a failed tank. When the relief valve is involved, we measure water pressure and check whether expansion has anywhere to go. We recommend nothing until the source is confirmed.

Once we know the source, the repair is usually straightforward. A new T&P valve, a brass drain valve in place of a weeping plastic one, fresh dielectric fittings or supply connectors, and an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve where pressure is to blame can all be handled on the spot. A corroded tank is different. No sealant or patch will hold, so we explain replacement honestly and walk through the choices. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Moisture or rust flakes under the burner access cover
  • Water collecting under or beside the heater
  • Rusty or discolored hot water paired with dampness at the base
  • Steady drips from the drain valve near the base
  • Higher water use with no obvious explanation
  • A T&P valve line that drips or releases water now and then
  • Water stains, soft flooring, or musty smells near the heater closet
  • Corroded pipe connections or damp flex lines above the heater

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Leak Repair?

Leaks look simple and often are not. Water can start at a top fitting, run down under the jacket, and show up at the base, and sweating tanks add confusion. A wrong part is not the only risk. Plugging a dripping relief valve removes the protection against excess pressure and heat, forcing a corroded connection can split it open, and electric heaters bring high-voltage wiring into a wet area. And if the tank itself is failing, no DIY fix will hold for long.

Experience turns a guessing game into a short, clear diagnosis. We isolate each possible source in turn, confirm it with fresh water on a dried surface, and measure static pressure so the fix actually lasts. Worn relief valves, weeping drain valves, and corroded connections are replaced on the spot, and pressure problems get a real solution, whether that is an expansion tank or a pressure-reducing valve. A rusted-through tank gets an honest replacement recommendation rather than a patch, and we can add a piped drain pan and a leak sensor to protect your home going forward.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush the tank once a year so sediment does not overheat the bottom and speed corrosion
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years; once it is used up, the tank starts to corrode
  • Have household pressure measured, since high pressure wears out relief valves and fittings faster
  • Make sure a closed plumbing system has a working expansion tank to absorb heated water
  • Install a drain pan piped to a safe spot, which matters most for attic, closet, and upstairs heaters
  • A quick monthly look at the base, valves, and top connections catches leaks early
  • A battery leak alarm beside the heater costs little and can catch a problem days sooner
